The idea

An intimate taberna, built for sharing.

Maré Social takes its name from the Portuguese word for tide — a rhythm that never sits still. The kitchen works the same way: cold and hot tapas move across the table in waves, followed by mains, sushi, and dessert, in whatever order the evening calls for.

The menu is guided by Henrique Sá Pessoa, the two-Michelin-starred Portuguese chef known for grounding refined technique in honest, produce-led cooking. Here that translates into josper-grilled seafood, cured meats, and a Nikkei sushi counter alongside it — Iberian and Japanese references sharing one table.

Where it comes from

Two coastlines, one table.

The menu doesn't pick a single country — it follows the chef's own path, from the Portuguese coast to the sushi counter.

Lisbon

Portuguese taberna cooking

Josper-grilled meats and seafood, cured pasterma, croquettes, and the caldeirada fish soup that anchors the hot tapas list — rustic dishes served with restaurant-grade precision.

Iberia & the Mediterranean

Sharing plates

Burrata, Portuguese salads, batatas bravas, and paella for two — food designed to be passed around the table rather than plated for one.

Nikkei

Japanese-Peruvian sushi & ceviche

Nigiri, sashimi, and inspired rolls sit alongside tiraditos and crudos — a nod to the Nikkei tradition of Japanese technique meeting South American citrus and chili.
